Encrypt & Password Protect PDF

Add an open password to your PDF to block unauthorized access. Encrypt contracts, invoices and sensitive documents and share them safely.

How to use: Encrypt & Password Protect PDF

  1. Upload the PDF: Choose the PDF you want to protect with «Choose file».
  2. Set a password: Type the password people will need to open the document. Pick a long one that is hard to guess.
  3. Encrypt: Click the encrypt button. The file is uploaded for processing and deleted when it finishes.
  4. Download the protected file: The new PDF opens only with the password. Share the password through a separate channel.

When is it useful?

  • Sending contracts and offers: Password-protect a contract before emailing it and pass the password on by phone or message.
  • ID and health documents: Protect personal papers such as ID copies, reports or lab results before you share them.
  • Payroll and financial statements: Stop unauthorised people from opening tables you send to an accountant or a bank.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I add a password to a PDF?

Upload your PDF, set an open password, and download the encrypted file. The document only opens with the password.

How secure is the encryption?

The document is encrypted with AES-256, the strongest standard available for PDF and far more robust than the legacy RC4 encryption. Your password is what ultimately decides security: pick a long, hard-to-guess one and share it only with authorized people.

Can I open the encrypted PDF in any app?

Yes. Standard PDF encryption is used, so Adobe Acrobat, browsers, and all common readers open it by asking for the password.

Can I remove the password later?

Yes. As long as you know the password, you can remove protection with the 'unlock PDF' tool.

Can I also block printing and copying?

Beyond the open password, you can restrict document permissions to prevent unauthorized printing and copying.

Can you recover the document if I forget the password?

No. We do not store the password and cannot recover it, so keep it somewhere safe — and keep your original unprotected file too.

Will an encrypted PDF open everywhere?

Current browsers and PDF readers ask for the password and open it. Very old programs may not support AES-256.
