How to Write a Cover Letter: Structure and Tips

A good cover letter doesn't repeat the CV; it says what the CV doesn't. A four-paragraph structure.

A cover letter is the short letter alongside your CV where you answer "why this job, why me". Rather than repeating the CV, it puts it in context.

A four-paragraph structure

  1. Opening: say which role you're applying for and what interests you, in a sentence or two.
  2. Evidence: link one or two requirements from the ad to a concrete result from your CV, with numbers and context.
  3. Motivation: why this company or role, and what it adds for you.
  4. Close: state that you'd welcome an interview, thank the reader and refer to your contact details.

Length and tone

Stay within one page; about 250–350 words is enough for most applications. Be warm but professional, and if you know the reader's name use it instead of a generic greeting.

What to avoid

  • Repeating your CV sentence by sentence.
  • Generic lines that could go to any company.
  • Claiming work you haven't done or skills you don't have.
  • Typos: read it aloud before sending.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a cover letter mandatory?

If the ad asks for one, yes. If not, a good one can still set you apart, especially for career changes or things your CV needs to explain.

How long should it be?

One page, around 250–350 words in four short paragraphs, is enough for most applications.

Does the AI make things up in the letter?

Prompt rules and code checks guard against it, and a number or concept that isn't in your CV is flagged. You should still check every sentence before sending.
