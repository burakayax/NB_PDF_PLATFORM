Month-End Invoice Batch: PDFs Ready for Your Accountant

The time saving is not only in the extraction. It comes from tidying the folder before and checking the table after. A practical month-end routine.

For small businesses and freelancers, month-end often means opening PDF invoices one at a time and keying them into a sheet. Batch processing handles the lot in one pass, and a little preparation makes the result far more reliable.

What batch solves

One invoice is easy. The pain is dozens in a row. AI Batch works through a folder of invoices in turn, extracts each one's data (invoice number, date, seller, amount, VAT) and merges everything into one CSV with a row per invoice.

  1. Bulk-upload invoices: Add your PDF invoices to the AI Batch tool, up to 25 files.
  2. Choose Extract mode: Start the run and watch live progress for each invoice.
  3. Download one CSV: All invoices merge into one table, ready for Excel or your accounting import.

Tidy the folder before you start

A month's invoices arrive as email attachments, in the downloads folder and as phone photos. Processing them as they are means an hour spent cleaning up the output.

  • Remove duplicate copies of the same invoice; double entry is the most common error.
  • Move non-invoice documents, such as delivery notes, quotes and bank receipts, to a separate folder.
  • Run photographed invoices through the Document Scanner first, because legibility improves noticeably.
  • Put dates in file names so you can match a row back to its invoice while checking.

Check the table with totals, not row by row

Reading dozens of rows one by one gives back the time you saved. Open the table in Excel and run a few aggregate checks instead: compare the amount column total with the period total, check VAT against the amounts, sort the dates and look for anything outside the period, and filter seller names for anyone unexpected. Opening the handful of rows that look odd is far more efficient than reading them all.

The invoices most likely to go wrong

  • Photographed invoices that are shadowed or skewed.
  • Foreign-language invoices with a different date order, such as month first.
  • Multi-line invoices, where the total may read correctly while the line breakdown is incomplete.
  • Documents with handwritten notes or stamps added.
  • Invoices with discounts, advances or credits, where sign errors show up.

Build the archive from the start

Invoices are needed not only at month-end but years later, in an audit or a warranty claim. Merge the month's invoices into one PDF, add page numbers and make it searchable, and you avoid hunting through a folder of separate files. One file per month means twelve files a year, an archive a person can actually manage.

Agree a format with your accountant

Accountants usually expect a particular column order, date format and file naming pattern. Settle that once in the first month and each later month takes minutes. Arrange the CSV columns to match and save that arrangement as a template.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many invoices can I process at once?

Up to 25 files in one run. For more, process in several rounds and stack the resulting tables underneath each other in Excel.

Do different sellers' invoice layouts cause problems?

Layouts vary, but invoice number, date, seller and amount fields are recognized. With very unusual templates it is worth comparing a few rows against their source.

Can I import the result into accounting software?

CSV output is compatible with the import format of most packages. If the column order differs, arranging it once in Excel and saving a template is enough.

Are the processed invoices stored?

Documents are not kept on the server after processing finishes. You should still keep the original invoices in your own archive to meet statutory retention periods.
