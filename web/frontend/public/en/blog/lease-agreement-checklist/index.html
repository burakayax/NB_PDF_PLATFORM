Lease Agreement Checklist: Deposit, Rent, Move-Out Traps

Most tenancy disputes begin at move-out and revolve around the deposit. A short list of clauses and one set of photos can prevent nearly all of them.

A lease can look standard and still hide the clauses that cause the biggest fights: how the deposit is returned, how the rent rises, and how a tenancy ends. Read these points before you sign, not after the first dispute.

The short checklist

  • Rent amount, payment day and method.
  • Rent increase rate and the index it is tied to.
  • Deposit amount and return conditions.
  • Term, renewal and termination clauses.
  • Who is responsible for dues, bills and repairs.
  • Eviction conditions and notice periods.
  • An inventory list and a record of existing damage.

Deposit: where disputes begin

Disagreements rarely happen at move-in. They happen at move-out, almost always about the deposit. If the agreement does not state when, under what conditions and with which deductions it is returned, the outcome depends on what each side remembers. Make sure three things are written: the return period, the situations that allow deductions, and that ordinary wear and tear is not deductible.

Record the condition on moving day

The answer to a deposit dispute is usually a few photos taken on day one. Photograph paint, flooring, kitchen units, bathroom fittings and any scratches or stains, and note the meter readings. Turn the images into a single PDF and attach it to an inventory list signed by both sides. That is far stronger evidence than memory two years later.

What is the rent increase tied to?

The increase clause is often one line, yet it governs the most money. Is it a fixed percentage or tied to an index, and which period's figures apply? Statutory caps are also judged against this clause. Clarify any ambiguity before signing, because it is far easier than arguing a year later.

Who pays for what?

  • Service charges: which items, such as heating, cleaning and the lift, fall to the tenant?
  • Major repairs to fixtures are usually the landlord's responsibility.
  • Maintenance of the boiler, air conditioning and appliances should be stated explicitly.
  • Whose name are the utility accounts in, and who transfers them at the end?
  • Permission to redecorate: changes made without written consent cause problems at move-out.

Term, renewal and notice

How and when an agreement ends matters as much as how it starts. Missing the notice period can commit you to another term you did not want. On the day you sign, put the notice date in your calendar.

Keep the signed copy findable

A lease is requested in many places, from housing benefit applications to tax filings. Merge photos of the signed copy into one PDF and make it searchable, so one keyword finds it years later. If you share it with a third party, permanently remove unnecessary details such as identity numbers.

Frequently Asked Questions

What happens with something the agreement does not mention?

General law applies to matters left unwritten, but the sides may read them differently. Writing the dispute-prone topics, such as deposit, charges and alterations, explicitly into the agreement is the safest route.

What should I watch when asking AI about the contract?

Answers rest on the document's own text, so do not expect certainty about something the document does not cover. After getting an answer, read the relevant clause yourself.

Are handwritten additions valid?

Additions the parties agreed and signed form part of the agreement. Make sure such additions appear identically and are initialled on both copies to prevent confusion.

Is a scanned copy of the agreement enough?

A legible scan is accepted for most applications. Even so, keep the signed original; the scan is for archiving and easy sharing.

What are the most important clauses in a lease?

Rent increase rate, deposit return conditions, term, eviction and termination clauses, and responsibility for dues and repairs.

Does AI give legal advice?
