Hiding ID Numbers and Addresses in a Document Before You Share It

Official documents carry your identity number and address, and the recipient often does not need either. Covering them is not the same as removing them.

An estate agent asks for proof of income. Your document carries your identity number, address, date of birth and salary. The agent needs only some of that, but sending the whole document is the easy option, so most people do exactly that. Once the details are out, they cannot be pulled back.

The common mistake: a black box

Most people draw a black rectangle over the detail in a PDF viewer. On screen the information disappears and the job looks done.

It is not. A box drawn on a PDF is a shape added on top of the document, and the text underneath stays exactly where it was. If the recipient selects that area and copies it, your identity number comes out intact, and that takes no technical skill.

Test it on your own document. Select the area you covered, copy it and paste into a text box. If the information appears, you have not redacted it, you have only covered it.

The same flaw applies to white boxes, highlighter strokes and images pasted over text. Invisible is not the same as deleted.

The right method

Real redaction removes the text from inside the document. Nothing remains in the covered area to copy, and searching returns nothing either.

  1. Add the document: Give the PDF to the redaction tool. If the text is searchable in the document, it works directly.
  2. Choose what to remove: Patterned data such as identity numbers, phone numbers and bank account numbers is found automatically. Tick the ones you want removed.
  3. Check and download: Your selections are stripped out of the document. In the downloaded file, try selecting and copying those areas, and nothing should come out.

Scanned documents behave differently

If your document was scanned or photographed, the writing is an image and there is no selectable text layer. That is both good and bad news.

The good news is that the text cannot be copied anyway. The bad news is that it is still readable by eye, so you still need to cover it, and the covering has to be applied to the image itself. Zoom into the redacted area in the downloaded file to confirm it is genuinely covered.

What to hide for whom

Over-redacting makes a document useless, and under-redacting does not protect you. A practical rule: anything the recipient does not need for the task can be hidden.

  • A government body: usually hide nothing. They will verify your identity, and an incomplete document gets rejected.
  • An employer: the identity number is often required, but family details, home address and health data usually are not.
  • Private parties such as agents, sellers or websites: identity number and date of birth are almost never needed.
  • Anything you will post publicly: do not share the document at all, because even a redacted document carries details that can identify you.

Sometimes send one page instead

Sometimes the cleanest answer is not to redact but to send only the page that matters. Rather than sharing an eight-page statement with six pages blacked out, pull out the single relevant page and send that. It is safer, and it makes the recipient's job easier too.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does drawing a black box in a PDF delete the information?

No. The box is a shape added on top, while the text underneath stays in place and can be selected and copied. You need a redaction tool that actually removes the data.

How do I know the information was really removed?

In the downloaded file, select that area, copy it and paste into a text box. If nothing comes out, the data has been taken out of the document.

Which details are detected automatically?

Patterned data such as national identity numbers, phone numbers and bank account numbers is detected automatically, and you choose from the list which ones to remove.

Should I hide my identity number on a document for a government body?

No. They will verify your identity, and a redacted document may be treated as incomplete and rejected. Redaction makes sense for private parties who do not need the data.

Does redaction work on scanned documents?

In a scanned document the writing is an image with no selectable text layer, so the covering is applied to the image itself. Zoom into the area after downloading to check it.
