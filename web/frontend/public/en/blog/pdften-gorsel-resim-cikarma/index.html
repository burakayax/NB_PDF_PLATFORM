Extract Images from a PDF: Save Embedded Photos Without Quality Loss

Want to save the pictures inside a PDF but keep losing quality with screenshots? Converting pages to images and extracting embedded images are different things — this guide separates them and shows the easiest way.

There's a useful photo, logo or graphic inside a PDF and you want it as a separate file. Taking a screenshot loses quality. The right way is to extract the actual embedded image in its original form — this post shows how.

Key difference: "PDF to image" ≠ "extract images from PDF"

These two jobs are often confused but they're different, and choosing the right tool decides the quality:

  • PDF to image: Takes a screenshot (JPG/PNG) of the entire page. Text, lines and pictures all flatten into one image.
  • Extract images from PDF: Doesn't rasterize the page; it pulls out the actual embedded image files (photos, logos) in their original format and resolution.

Want the whole page as an image? Use "PDF to Image". Want only the photos/logos inside at original quality? Use "Extract Images".

Method 1 — Screenshot (not recommended)

Opening the PDF and screenshotting the picture is the best-known but worst way: the result is limited to your screen resolution, cropping edges is hard, and original quality is lost.

Method 2 — Convert the page to an image

If the image fills the whole page, converting the page to a high-resolution image can work. But if the page also has text and other elements, they all blend into the image — you can't isolate just the photo.

Method 3 — Extract embedded images (the cleanest)

The most accurate result is to extract the actual embedded image streams. PDF Platform's Extract Images tool does exactly that:
