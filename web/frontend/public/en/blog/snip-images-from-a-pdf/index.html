Why PDF Screenshots Blur, and How to Snip Sharply

A screenshot is limited to your screen. A snip is drawn from the document at the resolution you choose, so charts and tables stay crisp on a slide or in print.

There is a table, chart, diagram or single question in a PDF, and you want it in a slide, a report or a lesson. The first instinct is a screenshot, and the result is usually a soft, crookedly cropped image. The better way is to cut the region straight out of the document at a resolution you choose.

Why a screenshot falls short

A screenshot copies your screen, so the image is limited to your screen's resolution rather than the PDF's.

  • Text and thin lines go soft and fall apart when you zoom in.
  • The difference shows immediately on a projector or in print.
  • Cropping the edges by hand is fiddly, with a pixel too much here and too little there.
  • Even zooming into the PDF first will not give you more than your screen has.

What a snip does instead

A snip is generated from the document, not from your screen. You select an area on the page and the tool redraws only that region at the resolution you picked. A table that looks small on screen can still become a print-quality image.

The resolution options are 1x, 2x and 3x. 1x is for screen use, 2x is plenty for print and slides, and 3x is for large output or blowing up a very small detail.

Six steps

  1. Add your PDF: Drop in your document. It never leaves your device.
  2. Pick the page: Use the page strip on the left to move to the page you want.
  3. Select the area: Drag a box on the page, then move it around and resize it from the corners.
  4. Set resolution and format: Choose 1x, 2x or 3x and PNG or JPEG. PNG suits text and graphics, and JPEG suits photos.
  5. Add the snip: It drops into the basket. Take as many snips as you like, from any page.
  6. Download: Save snips one by one, grab them all as a ZIP, or turn them into a print-ready A4 worksheet.

The snip basket

One snip is rarely enough: three charts from a report, five questions from a book. Every snip you take collects in the basket, and the page strip shows a badge with how many you took from each page. When you finish, download the lot in one go instead of handling files one at a time. The worksheet option lays your snips out on A4 pages in order, in one or two columns, ready to print. Reorder them in the basket and the sheet follows that order.

Where it helps

  • Moving a chart from a report into a slide deck.
  • Pulling one product shot out of a catalogue.
  • Collecting specific questions from lesson notes or a question bank.
  • Sharing a clause from a contract legibly instead of as a soft screenshot.
  • Lifting a logo or stamp cleanly out of an old document.

Snip, convert a page or extract images?

These are three different jobs, and knowing which one you want gets you the right result first time:

  • Snipping turns the region you choose into an image. A table, chart or paragraph, with you drawing the boundary.
  • PDF to image converts the whole page into a single image.
  • Extracting embedded images pulls out the original photo files stored inside the document. It does not capture page text or vector drawings.

Privacy and memory

Snipping runs entirely in your browser and your PDF is never uploaded. The whole page is not drawn at a huge scale either, since only the region you selected is rendered, which keeps it fast and easy on your device's memory.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I save a region of a PDF as an image?

Upload the PDF to the Snip PDF to Image tool, drag to select the area you want, and click Add snip. It is captured as a high-resolution image in your basket, ready to download individually or all together.

How is this different from a screenshot?

A screenshot is limited to your screen resolution. A snip is rendered straight from the PDF at the resolution you pick (2x or 3x), so text and lines stay sharp enough to print.

Can I get several snips in one file?

Yes. Download every snip as a single ZIP archive, or combine them all into one PDF document.

Should I pick PNG or JPEG?

PNG (lossless and sharp) for text, tables, charts and drawings, and JPEG (smaller file) for photo-heavy snips.

Is my document uploaded to a server?

No. Everything runs in your browser, and your PDF is never sent to the internet.

Is snipping from a PDF free?

Yes. It is free with no signup or installation and runs directly in the browser on computers, tablets and phones.
