Split Payslips into One File per Employee

The payroll system gives you one PDF holding forty payslips. Everyone needs to get only their own page, and doing that by hand is both slow and risky.

It is the start of the month, payroll has sent the file, and you have a single PDF: forty pages, forty employees. Each person should receive only their own page. Doing that by hand — saving and naming each page in turn — costs you half a day and carries a risk of things getting mixed up. If they do, the consequence is serious: one employee's salary has gone to someone else.

Why not do it by hand?

The real issue here is not time but error. Salary information is among the most sensitive personal data there is, and reaching the wrong person cannot be undone. In a repetitive manual task, losing focus by the fortieth page is entirely normal.

A method that does the job in one move brings the time down to minutes and removes the question of whether anything got mixed up.

Step by step: splitting one PDF into pages

  1. Add the payroll file: Give the PDF to the split tool. The work happens in your browser; salary data is not uploaded.
  2. Pick pages visually: Pages are listed as thumbnails, so you can confirm which page belongs to whom.
  3. Take each page as its own file: Pages are separated individually and downloaded in a ZIP; each page is its own file.
  4. Name the files: Before sending, give each file the employee's name. This step is your last defence against a misdirected email.

Split PDF — Break a combined payroll file page by page — on your device, with nothing uploaded.

When one employee spans several pages

In some payrolls a single employee takes two or three pages. Then splitting page by page will not do; you need page ranges.

The split tool lets you choose ranges: pages 1–2 as one file, 3–4 as another. Thanks to the thumbnails you can confirm each range by eye — far safer than typing numbers blind.

Always check before sending

Even when the split is correct, no tool can catch a slip in your sending list. Do these two checks first:

  • Open three files at random and confirm the name inside matches the file name.
  • Does the number of files match the number of employees? A file too few or too many means the ranges have slipped.

Do not send payslips as a group email. A single «reply all», or a wrongly chosen recipient list, can expose the whole company's salaries. Each payslip should go in its own email, to one recipient.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I turn every page of a PDF into its own file?

Yes. Pages are separated individually and downloaded in a ZIP, each page as its own file.

What if one person's payslip is two pages?

Split by page range instead: 1–2 as one file, 3–4 as another. The thumbnails let you confirm each range by eye.

Is salary data uploaded to your server?

No. Splitting runs in your browser (on your device); the file is never sent over the internet and no account is needed.

How can I be sure each file belongs to the right person?

Before sending, open a few files at random and check the name inside matches the file name, and confirm the file count equals the employee count.

How large a file can I split?

Up to 80 MB per file; there is no separate limit on the number of pages.
