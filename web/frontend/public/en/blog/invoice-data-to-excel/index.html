Invoice PDF to Excel: Totals and Lines Without Retyping

Retyping invoices is slow and every typo travels into your books. Here is how the three approaches compare and how to get a whole folder into one spreadsheet.

If your month-end includes copying numbers from PDF invoices into a spreadsheet, you know how much time it eats. This guide ranks the three ways of doing it, from slowest to fastest, and walks through the quickest one.

Why a PDF will not simply give you a table

A PDF is built to look the same everywhere, not to hand over data. Invoice numbers, dates, totals and line items look neat on the page but are not stored as a table. Every supplier also lays things out differently, so copy and paste usually lands values in the wrong cells.

Approach 1: type it in

Fine for a couple of invoices a month. Beyond that it is slow, repetitive and the errors are inevitable.

  • Good: needs no extra tools.
  • Bad: slow and prone to mistakes.
  • Best for: a few invoices a month.

Approach 2: OCR, then tidy by hand

A scanned invoice is a picture, so OCR has to turn it into text first. That gives you words, but you still have to arrange them into columns yourself.

For scanned PDFs, our OCR converts the image to real text on your device (Turkish and English), and the file is never uploaded.

Approach 3: let AI read the document

An AI that understands invoices can pick out the fields and line items for you. That is what the Extract Data tool does:

  1. Upload the PDF: Choose the invoice, delivery note or table. Text is read on your device, and OCR starts by itself for scans.
  2. Click Extract Data: The AI identifies the document type and returns fields such as invoice number, date, seller and buyer, tax ID, subtotal, VAT and total, plus any line items, as structured data.
  3. Review and export: See the fields and tables, then export to CSV for Excel or to JSON in one click.

From one invoice to a whole folder

The bigger gain is in batches. The batch tool processes many invoices and merges the results into one CSV, with a row per invoice.

  • Up to 25 files at once.
  • Live progress for each file.
  • All invoices' fields combine under one CSV header.

What leaves your device

The text of your PDF is extracted on your device and only that text goes to the AI; the file itself is not uploaded. Most of our structural tools, such as merge and compress, run entirely in your browser.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I get invoice data into Excel?

Upload the PDF to the Extract Data tool. The AI extracts invoice number, date, parties, totals and line items as structured data, and you export it to CSV for Excel in one click.

Does it work on scanned invoices?

Yes. For scanned or photographed invoices, OCR runs on your device to turn the image into text before the data is extracted.

Can I combine many invoices into one table?

Yes. The AI Batch tool processes dozens of invoices and merges them into one CSV table with one row per file.
