Tender Documents Risk Review: Check Before You Bid

Submitting a bid means accepting everything in the specification and draft contract, usually without negotiation. The review therefore has to happen before the bid.

In a tender the draft contract is normally take-it-or-leave-it. Your bid is treated as acceptance of the specification and the draft, so the real decision is made while you read the file, not when you sign. That makes the reading stage the place to spend your time.

Start with the documents against each other

A tender file typically holds an administrative specification, a technical specification, a draft contract and annexes. They are written by different people, and it is common for one to say 90 days and another 60. Find the clause that ranks the documents, and send a written clarification request for every conflict you find.

Eight points that carry the risk

  • Completion period and extensions: do force majeure and delays caused by the authority move the date?
  • Late penalty: daily rate, cap, and the amount it is calculated on.
  • Guarantees: size of the bid and performance bonds, when they are returned, when they are forfeited.
  • Payment: how often interim payments are made, the payment term, and whether late payment earns interest.
  • Price adjustment: is there any mechanism if input costs rise on a long job?
  • Variations: what limit applies when the authority increases or reduces the work?
  • Termination: when and how can the authority end the contract, and what does that cost you?
  • Missing annexes: documents the draft refers to that are not in the file.

Watch the precedence clause

Many drafts say which document wins if two conflict. That sentence can lift an innocent-looking line in an annex above the main contract, so read the annexes with it in mind.

Rules change, so verify them

Public tenders in Turkey are governed by the Public Procurement Law, the Public Procurement Contracts Law and the regulations under them, and these are amended from time to time. When judging whether a provision is lawful, read the current text at official sources such as the Official Gazette, mevzuat.gov.tr and the Public Procurement Authority. For an important tender, legal support also helps you keep the objection and application deadlines.

Clarification and objection windows are short. Reading the file in the first days, not the last, leaves room both to ask questions and to decide whether to bid.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can the draft contract be changed?

Usually not, since bidding means accepting it. Objections and clarification requests have to be made before you bid and within the stated time limits.

Does the Contract Auditor cover public tenders?

Yes. Tick the 'Public tender / specification' option and questions about compliance with procurement rules are also considered. The result is a preliminary assessment; for important tenders consult a specialist.

Yes. Text is read on your device with OCR (first 30 pages). PDFs with real text give faster and more accurate results.
