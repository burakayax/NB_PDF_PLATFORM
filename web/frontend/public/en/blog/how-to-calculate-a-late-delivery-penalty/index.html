Late-Delivery Penalty Calculator: Clause Into Numbers

A rate of 0.5% per day looks small until you multiply it out. This guide shows the arithmetic, where the traps hide and what to ask for before signing.

Most delay clauses fit in one line: 'for each day of delay, a given percentage of the contract price'. The line is short and the consequence can be large, so before you sign it is worth doing the sum once and seeing where the clause could take you.

The formula

Penalty = base amount x daily rate x days late. On a 1,000,000 TL contract at 0.5% per day, each day costs 5,000 TL. Twenty days is 100,000 TL, which is 10% of the price. The same 10% arrives in ten days if the rate is 1% instead.

How fast common rates add up

  • 0.1% per day: 1% after 10 days, 10% after 100 days.
  • 0.5% per day: 10% after 20 days, 50% after 100 days.
  • 1% per day: 10% after 10 days, the full price after 100 days.

Three questions that change the result

  1. Is there a cap? A sentence such as 'total penalties shall not exceed 10% of the contract price' is the single most useful protection. Without it the penalty can reach the whole price and beyond, and if the contract also lets the other side claim damages in addition, the clause is uncapped in practice.
  2. What is the base? 'Contract price' may mean the VAT-inclusive price, the VAT-exclusive price or only the delayed portion. If the contract does not say, expect the other side to pick the biggest figure; a VAT-inclusive base gives a penalty 20% higher than a VAT-exclusive one.
  3. Whose delay is it? If your deadline depends on an approval, sample or document the other side must provide, their delay should not count against you. Look for an extension clause for delays they cause, and ask for one if it is missing.

Do not count on a court to reduce it

There is a general principle that a court may reduce an excessive penalty, but commercial matters between merchants fall under a separate rule where that room may be limited. If you are a merchant, negotiating a heavy clause before signing is far safer than hoping to have it trimmed afterwards. Check the current wording at an official source such as mevzuat.gov.tr; this article is not legal advice.

Two requests are often accepted: a cap on total penalties, and mutuality, meaning a matching penalty or interest when the other side pays late.

Frequently Asked Questions

What daily rate counts as reasonable?

It depends on the sector and the job, so there is no single right figure. Judge the rate together with the cap, because even a small daily rate keeps growing when nothing limits it.

Can a penalty and damages both be claimed?

Yes, if the contract permits it. A clause such as 'if the penalty does not cover the loss, damages may be claimed in addition' removes the cap in effect, so look for that sentence before signing.
