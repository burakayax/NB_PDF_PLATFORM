How to Understand a Contract Written in a Language You Do Not Speak

You do not need to read every line. Translate, summarize, then spend your attention on the handful of clauses where the risk sits.

Contracts, proposals and technical documents often arrive in a language you do not read well. Translating every line with a dictionary is slow. A better routine is to translate the document with its structure intact, summarize it, and then concentrate on the few clauses that matter.

Translate, then summarize

Where to look first

Reading the whole contract is usually unnecessary, because the real risk concentrates in a few clauses. Most commercial contracts use similar headings:

  • Parties and authority: who signs, and does the document show their authority?
  • Term and renewal: does it renew automatically, and how much notice prevents that?
  • Termination: in what circumstances and with how much notice can it end?
  • Payment: amount, currency, due dates and late interest.
  • Liability cap and indemnity: is there an upper limit on damages?
  • Governing law and jurisdiction: where and under which law are disputes resolved?
  • Confidentiality and non-compete: duration and scope.

Why governing law deserves attention

The governing law and jurisdiction clause is near the end and often very short, yet in a dispute it can matter more than everything above it. Pursuing a claim in another country's courts can cost more than the work the contract covers. Do not skip it, and ask directly about anything unclear.

Mind the legal vocabulary

Some legal terms differ from everyday meaning, and a literal translation can mislead. In English contracts 'shall' expresses obligation while 'may' expresses discretion, and terms such as 'indemnify', 'warranty' and 'best efforts' carry meanings tied to a legal system. A translation tells you what the text says. Judging its legal effect is a separate job.

Comparing two versions

In negotiation the other side often sends an updated version and says the edits are minor. Rather than rereading everything, compare the two versions: added, removed and changed clauses appear one by one. This is how quiet changes to amounts, dates and liability get caught.

When you need a professional

AI translation and summaries help you understand a document and ask better questions. They do not replace a lawyer on contracts you will sign, that involve significant sums or that bind you for a long time. Official procedures require a sworn translation, and institutions do not accept machine translation.
