University Enrolment Documents the Portal Accepts First Time

Enrolment week is short and the system is busy. Most rejected uploads trace back to how the document was captured, not to the document itself.

Enrolment days are short and the system is busy, and you have a stack of documents: a school diploma, exam results, an ID copy, proof of address, a medical report and a photo. Most of it is uploaded digitally now. This guide covers how to prepare them so they are accepted first time.

Scan, do not photograph

This is the most common mistake. Photographing a document on a table is quick, but the result is usually rejected: the table shows, the edges are skewed, light hits one corner and the file is needlessly large.

A document scanner finds the edges, corrects the perspective and lays the paper down as a flat page. The result looks scanned and the file is far smaller, and you need no extra hardware because your phone camera is enough.

  1. Put the document on a flat surface: A dark surface makes the edges easier to find. Flatten the paper as much as you can.
  2. Light it from above, not the side: Keep your own shadow off the document. Window light is enough, and flash usually causes glare.
  3. Run the scanner: Edges are found automatically and the page is flattened. Adjust the corners by hand if needed.
  4. Save it as a PDF: You can gather several pages into one document.

Do not scan what a portal can issue

Some documents need no scanning at all. Proof of address, civil registry extracts and criminal record certificates can be downloaded straight from a government portal as PDFs. A downloaded document is already digital, sharp and small, and it can be verified by the barcode on it. Printing and rescanning throws all of that away.

Do not take screenshots. Use the download as PDF option, because verification barcodes usually do not survive a screenshot and the document may be rejected.

The photo

The photo for a student card has specific pixel dimensions and a file size limit. A phone photo is both the wrong ratio and larger than needed, so crop to passport-photo ratio first and then set the required size. If the dimensions fit but the file is still large, compress it separately, since pixel dimensions and file size are different things and most systems require both.

One file or several

Universities usually have a separate upload field per document. If a single file is wanted, order the documents as the enrolment guide lists them.

Where a document runs to several pages, such as both sides of a diploma, merge them into one PDF before uploading, because systems often accept only one file per field.

If you hit the upload limit

  • Compress the scanned documents, since that is where the gain is.
  • Scan in greyscale rather than colour. Without a coloured stamp or signature, colour is not needed.
  • After compressing, check the smallest text, because an unreadable document counts as a missing one.
  • Do not leave uploading to the last day. Enrolment systems are busiest then and errors are more likely.

A final check

  • Is each document fully in frame, with no clipped corners?
  • Does the text read without zooming?
  • Are the pages upright?
  • Is every item on the guide's list ready?
  • Are the file names clear? Something like diploma-name-surname.pdf works well.

Open the document on your own phone and read it before uploading. If you find it hard to read, the person at the other end will not manage at all.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I photograph documents with my phone and upload them?

Use a document scanner rather than taking a photo. It finds the edges, flattens the page and produces a much smaller, acceptable file.

Should I print and scan a document I downloaded from a portal?

No. The PDF you downloaded is already digital, sharp and verifiable. Printing and rescanning inflates the file and lowers the chance the barcode scans.

How do I upload both sides of a diploma?

Merge the two pages into one PDF and upload that, since systems usually accept only one file per field.

What if the file exceeds the size limit?

Compress the scanned documents and scan in greyscale. Afterwards, check the smallest text is still readable.

Are my documents uploaded to your server when scanning?

No. Document scanning and page merging run in your browser, on your device. Your files are never sent over the internet.
