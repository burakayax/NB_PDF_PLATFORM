Can You Trust AI to Review a Contract? How It Works and Where It Fails

Handing a contract to an AI is easy. Knowing how far to trust the answer is the hard part. This is a plain account of the process, the safeguards and the limits.

Whether an AI contract review is useful depends less on the model than on the process around it. Here is what the Contract Auditor does with your document, what stops it from inventing things, and the places where you should not rely on it.

What happens to your document

  1. Text is extracted on your device: The PDF itself is not uploaded. Only its text is sent for analysis, and scanned pages are read on your device with OCR first.
  2. The document is identified and you answer a few questions: The system works out the document type and the parties, then asks questions whose answers change the analysis. You say which side you are on; it does not guess.
  3. The analysis runs: A quick scan finds the main risks in one pass. A detailed audit reads the document several times, calculates figures, looks up legal points in official sources and puts the findings through a second check.
  4. The result appears in the document: Risky passages are highlighted in your PDF. Each finding gives the reason and the impact, and the detailed audit adds suggested replacement wording.

Four safeguards against made-up findings

  1. Every finding carries a word-for-word quote, and software searches for that quote in the document. A finding whose quote cannot be found never reaches the report.
  2. Page numbers come from where the quote really sits in the document, not from the model's estimate.
  3. In the detailed audit, statute articles are never written from the model's memory. Only text read from an official source is shown, with a link, and anything that could not be read is marked 'could not verify'.
  4. What the system does not know is listed openly in an assumptions section.

Where it can still fail

  • It can be wrong, either missing a risk or overstating one. The output is a preliminary assessment, not legal advice.
  • It cannot judge what it has not seen. An annex that is not in the file cannot be analysed, and the report says so.
  • Only the detailed audit checks current law; the quick scan gives no statutory basis.
  • Very long documents (above roughly 240,000 characters) have to be split, and for scans the first 30 pages are read.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does AI replace a lawyer?

No. The result is a preliminary assessment. For critical and high-risk clauses, consult a lawyer before signing; the report makes that conversation more efficient.

How far can I trust the findings?

Each finding's quote is verified in the document and its reason is stated, but interpretation errors are still possible. Confirm anything important against the document itself.

Can I reopen the result later?

No. The result is shown only on the page where the analysis finished and is deleted from the server within 30 minutes. Download the report and highlighted PDF right away.

What happens if the analysis fails?

The allowance or credits used are refunded.
