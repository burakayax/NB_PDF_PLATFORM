Combine PDF Files Into One: Free, No Upload

Merging is simple. Getting the order right and finishing the document properly is where the time goes. Steps, tips and the reason it can run without a server.

Contract annexes, scanned pages, a stack of reports: you want one file, not six. The merge tool does it inside your browser, so there is nothing to install and no account to make.

Why on-device merging is different

Most online tools send your files to their servers. Here the merge happens on your device and nothing goes over the internet. That helps privacy and speed alike: no upload wait, and the result is immediate.

Three steps

  1. Add your PDFs: Drag them in or select several at once. Up to 80 MB in total, with no limit on the number of files.
  2. Put them in order: Use the up and down arrows. The document is built in the order you see.
  3. Merge and save: Press Merge. One PDF is produced immediately and saved where you choose.

Ordering mistakes cost more than technical ones

Computers sort file names by characters, not by numbers, so annex-10 lands before annex-2. Look over the order once after adding the files; that is quicker than rebuilding a merged document.

  • Name annexes 01, 02, 10 so sorting behaves.
  • Cover or letter first, then annexes in the order the contract mentions them.
  • If the front and back of a document are separate files, check the sides line up.
  • A glance at each file's first page avoids adding the wrong one.

What to do after merging

A freshly merged file is rarely ready to send as it is. Page numbers, compression and a tidy first page change how it is received.

  • Add page numbers so the reader can see all annexes arrived.
  • Compress it if it is large, since merged scans quickly pass email limits.
  • Delete stray blank pages, especially scanned reverse sides.
  • Remove sensitive information permanently before sending.

Why it needs no server

Merging does not reprocess content. The page objects are simply rearranged into one file, which a browser can do alone. With no server involved there is nothing to meter, so there is no daily allowance, hourly cap or account. Word conversion and OCR genuinely need computation, so they run on a server. That is the difference.

Mixed page sizes and orientations

Putting a landscape table into an A4 contract leaves a file with mixed sizes. It is not an error and looks fine on screen, but a printer may scale pages differently. If you will print it, bring the pages to the same orientation before merging.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many files can I merge at once?

There is no limit on the number of files; the limits are total size and your device's memory. If the browser slows down on many large scans, do it in two rounds.

Does merging reduce quality?

No. Pages are not recompressed; they are carried into the new file as they are. The resulting size is roughly the sum of the inputs.

Are bookmarks kept in the merged document?

Page content is kept, but bookmarks and internal links from the source files may not always carry over. On long documents, check the contents section after merging.

Can I merge and delete pages in the same document?

Yes, but in order. Merge into one file first, then use the delete-pages tool for what you do not want; page numbers are easier to follow that way.

Do I need an account to merge PDFs?

No. The merge tool is free and needs no signup. It runs in your browser and your files are not uploaded.

What is the file size limit?

You can merge up to 80 MB in total on the web, with no limit on the number of files.

Are my files safe?

Yes. Merging happens on your device and your files are never sent to the internet.
