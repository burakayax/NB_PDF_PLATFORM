Extract Data from Invoices to Excel: 2026 Guide (3 Methods)

Typing invoice data into Excel by hand is slow and error-prone. This guide compares three methods and shows how to turn dozens of invoices into one table in seconds.

One of the biggest time sinks for accounting, procurement and finance teams: typing data from PDF invoices into Excel one by one. This post honestly compares three methods and shows the fastest, step by step.

Why is it hard?

PDF is a format designed for display — not for exporting data as a "table". Invoice numbers, dates, totals and line items look organized on the page but aren't stored as a clean table behind the scenes. And every vendor's layout differs, so copy-paste often produces broken alignment and wrong cells.

Method 1 — Manual entry

The familiar route: open the invoice and type values into Excel. Fine for one invoice, but for dozens it's slow and mistakes are inevitable.

  • Pro: no extra tools.
  • Con: slow, tedious, error-prone.
  • When: a few invoices a month.

Method 2 — OCR then clean up

For scanned (image) invoices you first need OCR to turn the picture into text, then structure it into a table by hand. OCR gives you text, but you still build the structure.
