When do you need to delete pages?

  • Cleaning up blank or duplicate pages a scanner added.
  • Removing internal/confidential pages before sharing a report.
  • Dropping repeated cover pages in a merged document.
  • Keeping only the section you need and removing the rest.

Deleting is not hiding — and hiding is not deleting

When you delete a page it genuinely does not appear in the new file; its content is not carried over. The point people confuse is different: if you want to hide a few lines on a page, drawing a black box over them is not enough. The box is added on top, while the text underneath stays in the file and can still be copied. Delete when the whole page must go; use a tool that removes content permanently when only part of a page must go.

Which pages should come out before sharing?

Pages nobody remembered to remove are the most ordinary form of leak. In corporate documents these are the ones that reach the other side unnecessarily:

  • Annexes holding internal pricing, cost or margin.
  • Template pages still carrying details of a previous client.
  • Signature routing sheets and internal approval forms.
  • Old version pages stamped as drafts.
  • Copies of identity or personnel documents holding personal data.

Typical leftovers from batch scanning

Scanners with automatic feeders save blank reverse sides as pages when scanning double-sided. A twenty-page document becomes forty pages and the file size doubles. Likewise, a page may have been scanned twice after a paper jam. Skimming the thumbnails and removing blanks and duplicates both shrinks the file and makes the document readable.

What to do after deleting

  • Existing page numbers no longer match the content; number after deleting, not before.
  • Page references in a contents section shift; check them on long documents.
  • If the document shrank a lot, compression may no longer be needed; measure once.
  • Save the trimmed version under a new name and keep the complete original in your archive.

When splitting beats deleting

If you will send five pages out of a hundred-page report, marking ninety-five pages makes no sense. Extracting the pages you need is far quicker there. The rough rule: keeping the majority means delete, keeping the minority means split.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a deleted page be recovered?

The page is not in the new file you downloaded. Since your original document is unchanged, though, you can always go back to it and start again.

Does deleting pages reduce file size?

Yes, because the deleted pages' content is not carried over. The gain is most noticeable on scanned documents, where dropping blank reverse sides shrinks the file considerably.

Can I delete pages from an encrypted PDF?

If the document is encrypted, its content cannot be read. Remove the password first and then delete pages.

Can I add new pages where I deleted some?

Rather than inserting directly, the practical route is to prepare the new pages as a separate PDF, merge it with the document, and then adjust the order.

Is deleting PDF pages free?

Yes. The Delete Pages tool is free and needs no signup; it runs in your browser and your file isn't uploaded.

Does deleting pages damage the original file?

No. Your original PDF stays unchanged; the tool creates a new copy with the selected pages removed.

Can I delete multiple pages at once?

Yes. Select as many pages as you like and remove them in one go.
