How to Convert a CV (Résumé) From Word to PDF the Right Way

Why you should send your CV as a PDF for job applications, and how to convert it from Word without breaking the formatting — step by step.

A CV is the first document an employer sees about you. A résumé built in Word can shift on different computers and its fonts can change. The fix is simple: convert it to PDF before sending.

Why should a CV be sent as a PDF?

  • Looks the same on every device: Fonts, line spacing and layout are preserved regardless of who opens it.
  • Professional standard: Most employers and career portals prefer, or even require, PDF.
  • Won't change by accident: A Word file can shift when opened; a PDF is fixed.

Convert a Word CV to PDF — step by step

  1. Upload your Word file: Drag and drop your .doc or .docx résumé into the Word-to-PDF tool.
  2. Convert: Click "Convert"; the PDF is prepared instantly with fonts, tables and layout preserved.
  3. Download and name it: Download the PDF and save it with a clean name like "First_Last_CV.pdf".

Final checks before you send

  • Is the file name professional? Use "First_Last_CV.pdf" instead of "document1.pdf".
  • Do the fonts and alignment in the PDF match the Word version?
  • Are the photo and links (LinkedIn, email) in the right place?
  • If the file is too big, shrink it to email size with "Compress PDF".

If you're asked to send several documents (CV + cover letter + certificates) as one file, convert them to PDF and then combine them with "Merge PDF".
