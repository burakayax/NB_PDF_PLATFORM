Merging e-Government Documents Without Breaking Their Barcodes

Merging is easy. The subtlety is that every barcoded document must stay verifiable, which rules out screenshots, rescanning and heavy compression.

An institution wants proof of address, a civil registry extract, a criminal record certificate and a social security statement in a single file, and the portal gives you each as a separate PDF. Merging them takes a minute, but barcoded documents need one piece of care.

The barcode is the document's identity

Documents downloaded from Turkey's e-Government portal carry a barcode or QR code with a verification code. The receiving institution scans it, or types the code into the portal's verification page, to confirm the document really was issued in your name.

One rule therefore governs everything you do: the barcode must stay readable. Cropping, heavy compression or rescanning at low resolution can make it unreadable, and then the document counts as invalid.

Never print an e-government document and scan it again. The PDF you have is already digital and sharp, and printing and rescanning inflates the file and lowers the chance the barcode scans. Use the original PDF as it is.

Four steps

  1. Download each document as a PDF: Use the download as PDF option rather than screenshots, because barcodes usually do not survive a screenshot.
  2. Add them to the merge tool: Select the files in one go. Merging runs in your browser, so your documents are not uploaded.
  3. Put them in the requested order: Drag the files in the list to reorder. If an order was specified, follow it, and if not, lead with the most important document.
  4. Merge and download: Open the single PDF and check quickly that everything is there and in the right order.

If the file is too large

Official portal documents are usually small, and together they rarely pass a few megabytes. An unexpectedly large file almost always means a scanned document slipped in, such as a photocopied diploma or a scan of an ID card.

Shrink only that scanned document, then merge. That way you never touch the sharpness of the portal's own output.

Personal data: think before you share

Official documents carry identity numbers, addresses and dates of birth. If the document goes to a government body, those details are required and should not be hidden.

If it goes to an estate agent, a prospective employer or a website, the situation differs, and covering the fields the recipient does not need is a reasonable precaution.

Do not redact by drawing a black box over the text. A box drawn on a PDF is only a picture, and the text beneath it stays selectable and copyable. Use a tool that truly removes the data.

A final check

  • Is every document inside the file? Compare the page count against what you downloaded.
  • Do the barcodes look sharp? Zoom in and check.
  • Have any documents expired? Institutions usually want recent criminal record and address certificates.
  • Is the file name clear? Something like application-documents-name-surname.pdf helps the reader.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does merging break the validity of official documents?

No. Gathering pages into one file does not touch their contents. The barcode and verification code stay exactly as they were and remain verifiable.

Are my documents uploaded when merging?

No. PDF merging runs in your browser, on your device. Your files are never sent over the internet and no account is needed.

How do I change the order of documents?

After adding the files, drag them into the order you want in the list. The new document is built in that order.

Can I merge screenshots instead?

It is not advisable. Verification barcodes usually do not survive a screenshot and the document may be rejected. Use the portal's download as PDF option.

What if the file size is large?

Usually a scanned document that slipped in is the cause. Compress only that one and then merge, leaving the portal's own output untouched.
