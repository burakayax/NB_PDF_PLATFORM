AI Summaries: Key Points From a 50-Page Contract

You rarely need every page; you need to know what it says and what to watch for. Here is how to get that quickly and how far to trust it.

When a long contract, tender or report arrives, the real question is simple: what does it say, and what should I watch for? A good AI summary answers that in seconds instead of an hour of reading.

More than a shorter copy

A useful summary does not just cut words. It recognizes the type of document and brings forward what matters. The AI Summarize tool distinguishes tenders, contracts, academic papers and financial reports, and highlights parties, key dates, amounts and obligations.

How to run it

  1. Upload the PDF: Text is read on your device, with OCR for scans. Your file is not uploaded; only the text goes to the AI.
  2. Click Generate Summary: You get a structured summary with title, parties, key points, critical dates and amounts, and takeaways.
  3. Download or share: Save the summary as a clean PDF or pass it on.

Prepare the input

A summary is only as good as the text the model sees. Giving it the relevant section of a two-hundred-page file produces a shorter and more accurate result than giving it everything. Separate annexes from the main text, remove unrelated periods and repeated boilerplate, and turn scans into clean pages first.

Verify before you rely on it

A summary shows you where to read; it does not replace reading. For any decision, check the critical facts against the document. The list is short and predictable:

  • Amounts and currency.
  • Dates, durations and notice periods.
  • Party names and signing authority.
  • Penalty, security and termination conditions.
  • Negative provisions such as 'excluded', 'not included' and 'limited to', which fade easily in a summary.

Each document type answers a different question

In a tender, the submission deadline and the security amount should stand out. In a financial report, look for period comparisons and movement in line items. A contract turns on parties and obligations, and a technical report on method and findings. Asking what actually matters in this kind of document makes gaps obvious.

When a summary is only a starting point

Contracts you will sign, notices with legal effect, medical reports and correspondence with public bodies belong here. The summary tells you which clause to discuss and with whom, and the right move is to take it to the relevant expert rather than decide on the summary alone.

Then ask questions

A summary gives the overall picture, but usually one concrete question remains, such as the cancellation fee or whether a clause binds you. Asking the document directly is quicker, because the answer rests on its own text and points to the relevant section.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the whole document sent to the AI?

The file itself is not sent. Text is extracted on your device and only the text is processed. Even so, with sensitive documents it is a good habit to remove sections you would rather not share beforehand.

How long is the summary?

Length follows the complexity of the document. The aim is enough information to decide, not a fixed word count, so very long documents naturally produce a fuller summary.

Will summarizing the same document twice give a different result?

The wording can vary, but the key facts drawn from the document stay the same. For critical use, rely on the relevant clause rather than the summary.

Can I summarize many documents at once?

Yes. Batch processing runs through a folder of documents and gathers the results into one file, which saves most of the time on large proposal or literature reviews.

How do I summarize a PDF with AI?

Upload the PDF to the AI Summarize tool. It detects the type and produces a structured summary with parties, dates, amounts and takeaways, which you can download as a PDF.

Can I summarize a scanned document?

Yes. OCR runs automatically for scanned documents to turn the image into text, and then the summary is generated.

Is the summary reliable?

The summary relies only on the document's content, and the tool is instructed not to state data it is not sure about.
