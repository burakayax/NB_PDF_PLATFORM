Hospitality & Restaurant Worker CV Example

In a hospitality & restaurant worker CV, write results rather than a duty list. Summary, bullet and skills examples inside.

The most common mistake in a hospitality & restaurant worker CV is listing duties instead of results. Employers want to see how well you did the job, not just that you were assigned it. This guide shows how to write the profile summary, work-experience bullets and skills section for a hospitality & restaurant worker role, with examples you can copy and adapt.

Order and length

Put your name, title and contact details at the top, a short profile summary beneath, then work experience, education and skills. With little experience (recent graduate, internship) move education and projects ahead of work history. One page is enough for most applications; a second page is acceptable beyond roughly ten years of experience.

Applicant tracking systems (ATS) read plain, single-column CVs with standard headings (Summary, Experience, Education, Skills) best. Pick a more designed template for the version you hand to a recruiter.

Profile summary examples for a hospitality & restaurant worker

Keep it to two to four sentences: years of experience, strengths and goal. Take one draft below and replace each [X] with your own information:

  • Friendly, fast service professional who prioritises guest satisfaction and stays calm and team-minded at peak hours.
  • I have [X] years in restaurants/hotels and I am committed to hygiene standards and courteous service.
  • I try to sense what guests need before they ask; with my menu and product knowledge I make the right suggestions and aim for a memorable experience.
  • I am used to shift work and a busy pace, I work in harmony with my teammates and I don't shy away from responsibility.
  • An organised, hygiene-minded service professional who can communicate with international guests.

Work-experience bullets for a hospitality & restaurant worker

Start each bullet with an action and, where you can, include a number: how many people, items, what percentage, how fast. Wherever you see [X] below, insert your own real figure; if you don't know it, leave that bullet out.

  • Served an average of [X] guests per day during busy shifts
  • Raised upselling by [X]% through menu knowledge
  • Handled cash and payments without errors
  • Maintained table setup, cleanliness and hygiene standards
  • Resolved guest complaints on the spot
  • Supported onboarding of new staff
  • Took reservations, planned table layouts and shortened waiting times
  • Served international guests in English and met their orders and needs
  • Worked on busy event and banquet service during peak seasons
  • Coordinated with the bar and kitchen so orders were served on time
  • Helped with stock and product counts and reduced waste
  • Managed check-in/check-out and guest requests
  • Followed food-safety and hygiene rules without exception

Only write what you actually did. If you couldn't explain a bullet in an interview, it shouldn't be on your CV.

Skills section for a hospitality & restaurant worker

Mirror the job ad's wording: if it says "Excel", write "Excel" rather than "office software". These are commonly requested for this role; select only what you genuinely know:

  • Guest Service
  • Teamwork
  • POS Operation
  • Hygiene Standards
  • Quick Decision-Making
  • Upselling

Checklist before you send it

  1. Fill in the numbers: No [X] placeholder should remain anywhere.
  2. Check the dates: No overlaps or unexplained gaps; the Analysis tab in CV Maker calculates this for you.
  3. Tailor to the ad: Where true, use the ad's keywords; the job-ad matcher shows which are missing.

Frequently Asked Questions

How should I present languages and shift work?

List languages with your level (e.g. "English: intermediate, spoken"). If you worked seasonally, give the periods, the shift pattern and the scale you served (tables, rooms). Don't overstate your level.

How long should a hospitality & restaurant worker CV be?

One page is enough for most applications. With a long work history you can go to two, but the most important content (summary, latest role, key skills) should be on the first page.

What should a hospitality & restaurant worker CV include if I have no experience?

Lead with education, internships and projects, volunteering and certificates. For each, say what you did and the result. Don't add experience or figures you don't have.
