Engineer CV Example

In a engineer CV, write results rather than a duty list. Summary, bullet and skills examples inside.

The most common mistake in a engineer CV is listing duties instead of results. Employers want to see how well you did the job, not just that you were assigned it. This guide shows how to write the profile summary, work-experience bullets and skills section for a engineer role, with examples you can copy and adapt.

Order and length

Put your name, title and contact details at the top, a short profile summary beneath, then work experience, education and skills. With little experience (recent graduate, internship) move education and projects ahead of work history. One page is enough for most applications; a second page is acceptable beyond roughly ten years of experience.

Applicant tracking systems (ATS) read plain, single-column CVs with standard headings (Summary, Experience, Education, Skills) best. Pick a more designed template for the version you hand to a recruiter.

Profile summary examples for a engineer

Keep it to two to four sentences: years of experience, strengths and goal. Take one draft below and replace each [X] with your own information:

  • Analytical engineer able to run projects both on site and in the office, with a focus on safety, quality and deadlines.
  • I have [X] years of engineering experience and I am strong in planning, technical drawing and team coordination.
  • I approach technical problems systematically and decide based on data, and I am used to working with cross-disciplinary teams.
  • I have taken responsibility at every project stage, from design to implementation and from cost to quality, and I care deeply about occupational safety.
  • An engineer eager to learn new technologies and improve processes, who is not afraid of responsibility.

Work-experience bullets for a engineer

Start each bullet with an action and, where you can, include a number: how many people, items, what percentage, how fast. Wherever you see [X] below, insert your own real figure; if you don't know it, leave that bullet out.

  • Delivered a project with a budget of [X] on time and within budget
  • Raised production efficiency by [X]% through process improvements
  • Prepared and submitted technical drawings and specifications
  • Coordinated a field team of [X] people
  • Audited compliance with safety and quality standards
  • Handled supplier selection and cost analysis
  • Prepared the project schedule and tracked progress with weekly reports
  • Checked site work for compliance with drawings and specifications
  • Cut production defects by [X]% with root-cause analysis
  • Tracked subcontractor and supplier performance and prevented schedule slips
  • Took part in health and safety meetings and contributed to risk assessments
  • Carried out calculations, simulations and tests
  • Kept quality documents and procedures up to date

Only write what you actually did. If you couldn't explain a bullet in an interview, it shouldn't be on your CV.

Skills section for a engineer

Mirror the job ad's wording: if it says "Excel", write "Excel" rather than "office software". These are commonly requested for this role; select only what you genuinely know:

  • AutoCAD
  • Project Management
  • MS Project
  • Quality Management (ISO 9001)
  • Occupational Safety
  • Cost Analysis
  • Excel

Checklist before you send it

  1. Fill in the numbers: No [X] placeholder should remain anywhere.
  2. Check the dates: No overlaps or unexplained gaps; the Analysis tab in CV Maker calculates this for you.
  3. Tailor to the ad: Where true, use the ad's keywords; the job-ad matcher shows which are missing.
  4. Export a PDF: Send a PDF with selectable text; add a photo only if the ad or local custom expects one.
