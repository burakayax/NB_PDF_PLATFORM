Microsoft Lens Alternative: Scan on Your Phone

Lens is gone and Microsoft suggests OneDrive. If you would rather not put every scanned page in a cloud, there are other routes, starting with what your phone already has.

Microsoft Lens, formerly Office Lens, stopped creating new scans on 9 March 2026, a month after it left the app stores. If it is still installed you can open old scans but not make new ones, and a habit many people had for a decade ended quietly.

The timeline

  • 9 January 2026: retirement announced and support ended.
  • 9 February 2026: removed from the App Store and Google Play.
  • 9 March 2026: new scans stopped working.

Microsoft's suggested replacement is the scanning feature in the OneDrive app.

Why OneDrive is not for everyone

It works, but it needs a Microsoft account and sends every scan to the cloud. That is fine for a receipt. For an ID, a medical report, a tenancy agreement or a client's paperwork, it is a different decision.

There is also a simpler complaint: signing in to a cloud service just to photograph a sheet of paper is a bigger answer than the problem needs. Lens was liked because it was small. Open, shoot, PDF.

Check what your phone already does

  • iPhone: in Notes, start a new note, tap the camera icon and choose Scan Documents. It detects edges, corrects perspective and works entirely on the device.
  • Android: in Google Drive, tap the plus button and choose Scan. The result is uploaded to Drive.
  • Many Samsung phones offer to scan from the camera app when you point it at a document.

For the occasional single page, these are plenty and you need to install nothing. You can stop reading here.

Where built-in scanners run out

They tend to struggle in predictable places: joining several pages neatly, sharing the result as a PDF, making the text searchable, and repeating the job from a computer. Some workplaces also forbid installing apps on phones.

A scanner that runs in the browser fits those gaps: nothing to install, no account, and the document never leaves the device.

The PDF Platform document scanner

It opens in your phone's browser. Point the camera at a document and the edges are found automatically, an angled shot is flattened, contrast is lifted and a PDF is produced. The camera frame, the edge detection and the PDF generation all run on your device, so the document is not sent anywhere.

  • Free: camera scanning, automatic edge detection, perspective correction, contrast improvement, 3 pages per scan and PDF download. No account needed.
  • Pro: unlimited pages, automatic capture (it takes the shot when you hold the phone steady), shadow removal and searchable PDF (OCR).

Three steps

  1. Open it in your phone browser: Nothing to download. Open the page, tap Scan document and allow camera access.
  2. Point at the document: Edges are found automatically, and shooting from an angle is fine because the result is flattened.
  3. Download the PDF: Several pages join into one PDF, and you can share it straight from the phone.

What you give up

  • No sync between devices. Scans do not pile up in a cloud; you save or share the PDF yourself.
  • It is not a store-installed app. You can add the site to your home screen and use it like one.
  • You need a connection the first time. Afterwards the scanning works offline, because the work was always on your device.

If you want scans collected in a cloud, Microsoft's OneDrive suggestion is the right call. If you want documents to stay on your phone, this is.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why was Microsoft Lens discontinued?

Microsoft retired the app. It was removed from the app stores on 9 February 2026 and stopped creating new scans on 9 March 2026. Microsoft recommends the scanning feature in the OneDrive app instead.

What happens to my old scans?

If the app is still installed you can keep opening old scans; you just cannot create new ones. It is worth moving anything important somewhere else.

Can I scan documents without installing an app?

Yes. The PDF Platform document scanner runs in your phone's browser. Open the page and allow camera access. No install, no account.

Is my scanned document uploaded to a server?

No. The camera frame, automatic edge detection and PDF generation all run on your device; the document is never sent to the internet.

How many pages can I scan on the free plan?

Three pages per scan. Unlimited pages, automatic capture, shadow removal and searchable PDF (OCR) are on the Pro plan.

Can I scan from a computer too?

Not with a camera directly, but you can upload photos from your computer and turn them into a document PDF.
