Pages scanned in the wrong order? Want to move a page to the front or back? Here's the easiest way to rearrange PDF pages — entirely in your browser.

Documents that come out of a batch scanner in reverse, a page that needs to be inserted later, or just a more logical flow… reordering PDF pages is far easier than you might think.

When do you need to reorder pages?

  • Fixing pages a batch scanner delivered in reverse order.
  • Moving an annex or cover to the front/back of the document.
  • Tidying the flow after merging several documents.
  • Putting sections in a logical order before a presentation.

The classic ordering fault of batch scanners

The most common problem with automatic feeders is double-sided documents coming out in the wrong order. The device scans all the fronts first, then all the backs after you flip the stack, leaving pages arranged 1, 3, 5… 6, 4, 2. The document looks unreadable but the content is complete; all that is needed is to fix the order. Knowing this saves you from rescanning.

An easy way to plan the order

Moving pages one at a time through a long document is tiring. The faster method is to settle the target order first — which section opens, where the annexes go, where the signature page sits — and then move pages in large blocks rather than individually. The numbers on the thumbnails let you see where you are as you go.

Two things people forget afterwards

  • Page numbers: if numbers are printed into the document, they no longer match. Number after reordering, not before.
  • Contents pages and cross-references: phrases like «see page 12» shift, and long documents need a review.

If printing and binding are involved

When a document will be printed double-sided, starting each section on an odd page is the usual convention, with a blank inserted where needed. For a document folded into a booklet, the page order is not the reading order at all; sheets are arranged so that folding produces the correct sequence. Leaving that imposition to the page layout tool is both faster and less error-prone than working it out by hand.

Reorder or merge?

Rearranging pages inside one document and bringing several documents together are different jobs that often get confused. If you have separate files, the right order is to merge them into one document first and then rearrange. If you are working inside a single file, reordering alone is enough.

Review before downloading

A reordering can look correct when you only check the sequence of numbers; the real check is the flow of content. Glancing at the first page, the section transitions and the last page before downloading prevents sending a misordered document.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I delete pages while reordering?

Reordering and deleting are separate tools. The practical order is to delete unwanted pages first and then arrange what remains, so you handle fewer pages.

Is reordering hard on long documents?

Because pages appear as numbered thumbnails, you can move a page by typing its target position even in a hundred-page document; dragging one by one is not required.

Does reordering change the content?

No. The pages themselves stay as they are and only their position changes. Text, images and formatting are preserved.

If a scan came out in the wrong order, should I rescan?

There is no need. If the content is complete, fixing the order is enough; rescanning is wasted time.

How do I change the order of PDF pages?

Upload the PDF to the Organize pages tool, move pages up/down or type a position number, then download the rearranged file.

Will my original file be damaged?

No. The original PDF stays unchanged; the tool creates a new copy in the new order.

Is it free?

Yes, free and no signup; the work happens in your browser.
