How to Rearrange PDF Pages and Fix Backwards Scans

Two-sided scans often arrive as 1, 3, 5 and then 6, 4, 2. The content is all there; only the order needs fixing. Here is how to do it quickly.

A batch scanner that delivers pages in reverse, an annex that belongs at the front, a flow that makes more sense in a different sequence: rearranging PDF pages is much easier than most people expect.

When you need it

  • Fixing pages a batch scanner delivered in reverse order.
  • Moving an annex or cover to the front or back.
  • Tidying the flow after merging several documents.
  • Putting sections in a logical order before a presentation.

Three steps

  1. Open the PDF: Drop your file into the Organize pages tool. Pages appear as numbered thumbnails.
  2. Set the new order: Move pages with the up and down buttons, or type a target position number. The new order shows instantly.
  3. Download: Confirm and download the rearranged PDF. Your original stays unchanged.

The batch-scanner order fault

Automatic feeders often mishandle double-sided documents. The device scans all the fronts first, then all the backs after you flip the stack, leaving pages arranged 1, 3, 5 and then 6, 4, 2. The document looks unreadable but the content is complete, and all it needs is a corrected order. Knowing this saves you from rescanning.

Plan the order before you move anything

Moving pages one at a time through a long document is tiring. Decide the target order first, meaning which section opens, where the annexes go and where the signature page sits, and then move pages in large blocks rather than individually. The numbers on the thumbnails tell you where you are as you go.

Two things people forget

  • Page numbers: if numbers are printed into the document, they no longer match. Number after reordering, not before.
  • Contents pages and cross-references: phrases like 'see page 12' shift, so long documents need a review.

If the document will be printed and bound

For double-sided printing, starting each section on an odd page is the usual convention, with a blank inserted where needed. For a booklet, the page order is not the reading order at all, because sheets are arranged so that folding produces the right sequence. Leave that imposition to a page layout tool; it is faster and less error-prone than working it out by hand.

Reorder or merge?

Rearranging pages inside one document and bringing several documents together are different jobs. If you have separate files, merge them into one document first and then rearrange. Inside a single file, reordering alone is enough.

Review before downloading

An order can look right when you only check the sequence of numbers, but the real test is the flow of content. Glance at the first page, the section transitions and the last page before downloading, so an out-of-order document never goes out.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I delete pages while reordering?

Reordering and deleting are separate tools. The practical order is to delete unwanted pages first and then arrange what remains, so you handle fewer pages.

Is reordering hard on long documents?

Because pages appear as numbered thumbnails, you can move a page by typing its target position even in a hundred-page document. Dragging one by one is not required.

Does reordering change the content?

No. The pages themselves stay as they are and only their position changes. Text, images and formatting are preserved.

If a scan came out in the wrong order, should I rescan?

There is no need. If the content is complete, fixing the order is enough, and rescanning is wasted time.

How do I change the order of PDF pages?

Upload the PDF to the Organize pages tool, move pages up or down or type a position number, then download the rearranged file.

Will my original file be damaged?

No. The original PDF stays unchanged, and the tool creates a new copy in the new order.

Is it free?

Yes, it is free with no signup, and the work happens in your browser.
