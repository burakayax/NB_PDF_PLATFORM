Reduce a Photo to 20, 50 or 100 KB for Application Forms

A phone photo easily weighs a few MB, while application forms often ask for 20, 50 or 100 KB. Here is how to hit the target without guessing at quality percentages.

You filled in the application form, you are uploading the photo, and the screen says the file is too large. A phone photo easily weighs several megabytes, while forms often set a tiny limit such as 20, 50 or 100 KB. The fix is not cropping the photo; it is compressing the file below the limit.

KB versus MB

1 MB is 1024 KB. Taking a 3 MB photo down to 100 KB therefore shrinks the file roughly thirtyfold. Some quality is traded for that, and the aim is to keep the loss below what the eye can see.

Target-size mode: no quality guesswork

The classic way is to guess a quality percentage, download, check the size and start over if it misses. In the target-size mode of Compress Image you skip all that: type the target and the tool finds the highest quality that stays under it.

  1. Add your photo: Drop the image into the tool. It is processed in your browser and never sent to a server.
  2. Choose «To a target size»: There are one-click buttons for 20 KB, 50 KB, 100 KB, 200 KB, 500 KB and 1 MB. For any other figure, type it in KB.
  3. Read the result screen: The tool states which quality was used as a percentage and explains what it means. If the dimensions had to be reduced, it says so plainly.
  4. Download and upload: If the file is under the target, upload it straight to the application screen.

Compress Image — Type the target size and we pick the quality — your photo never leaves your device.

How much quality is lost?

You can read the quality percentage the tool reports like this: above 80% the loss is invisible. Below 45% text and fine detail start to degrade. For photos where the face matters, open the result at full size and check that the face is still sharp.

When the target cannot be reached

A very small target (say 20 KB) combined with a very large photo may need more than lower quality. The tool then says so plainly and hands you the smallest version it achieved; it never quietly gives you a file above the target. Two ways forward:

  • First lower the pixel dimensions with Resize Image, then compress. A smaller size takes far fewer KB at the same quality.
  • Raise the target slightly, if the form allows it.

Common mistakes

  • Cropping the photo to make it smaller. Cropping permanently discards part of the picture and is usually unnecessary; target-size mode keeps the whole image.
  • Compressing the same photo again and again. Every lossy save costs a little more quality. Always start from the high-quality original.
  • Mixing up pixel size and file size. A size such as 300 × 400 and a limit such as 60 KB are separate requirements, and you must meet both.
  • Using a screenshot instead of a photo. Screenshots are often larger than needed and the wrong size.

Crop first, resize second, compress last. That order avoids squashing the face and needless quality loss.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I reduce a photo to 100 KB?

In Compress Image, upload the photo, switch to «To a target size» and click 100 KB. The tool finds the highest quality that stays under 100 KB.

Can I get down to very small targets like 20 or 50 KB?

There are one-click buttons for 20 KB and 50 KB too. With a very large photo, quality alone may not be enough; the tool then says so plainly and suggests lowering the pixel dimensions first.

How many KB is 1 MB?

1 MB is 1024 KB.

Should I crop the photo to make it smaller?

No. Cropping permanently discards part of the picture. Target-size mode shrinks the file while keeping the whole image.

What if the target cannot be reached?

The tool says so plainly and gives you the smallest version it achieved. You can then lower the pixel dimensions or raise the target slightly.

Is my photo uploaded to a server?

No. Compression runs entirely in your browser; your photo is never sent to the internet and no account is needed.
