Check the photos before you send

Images go into the PDF as they are; the tool does not fix the photo. So the real quality difference comes from the shot, not the conversion. If you are preparing an application or an official file, reviewing the points below beforehand is faster than sending it twice.

  • Keep all four edges of the document in frame; a cut-off edge gets files rejected.
  • Avoid shadows and glare; light the page from the front rather than the side.
  • Shoot straight on; a skewed photo stays skewed in the PDF.
  • Make sure the text is legible; if you struggle on screen, so will the recipient.

If your photos are skewed or shadowed, the Document Scanner gives a better result than converting directly: it detects the edges, corrects perspective, and the output looks like it came from a scanner.

Why page order matters

Your phone gallery sorts photos by capture time, which is often not the document's logical order. Fronts and backs of double-sided paperwork get mixed up especially often. Reviewing the order once end to end after arranging avoids the reply that says the pages are out of sequence.

If the file comes out large

A single photo from a recent phone can be 3-5 MB. Ten pages of paperwork then reach 30-50 MB and exceed the 20-25 MB attachment cap on most email services. The fix is not to degrade the shots but to run the finished PDF through the compress tool; gains on scanned material are usually substantial.

Making the PDF searchable

In a PDF built from images the text is really a picture: you cannot search it or copy it. If you need search, run the PDF through OCR; an invisible text layer is added and the document becomes findable with Ctrl+F, while its appearance stays the same.

JPG or PNG?

JPG suits photographed paperwork and keeps the file small. For screenshots, charts and images with sharp lines, PNG looks cleaner because its compression is lossless, at the cost of size. Mixing both in the same PDF causes no problem.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will the order of images change in the PDF?

The order you set in the tool is preserved, and each image becomes its own page. You can drag to reorder after uploading and check it before downloading.

The PDF came out very large — what should I do?

Phone photos are several MB each, so the total grows fast. Running the finished PDF through the compress tool cuts the size noticeably without hurting legibility.

Can I search the text in the PDF afterwards?

Not directly; on a page built from an image the text is a picture. Run it through OCR to add an invisible text layer, and search will work.

Can images of different sizes go into the same PDF?

Yes. Each image becomes its own page, so pages may differ in size. If you plan to print, resizing the images to a common size first gives a tidier result.

How do I convert images to PDF?

Add JPG/PNG files to the Image → PDF tool, set the order and download one PDF. Each image becomes a page.

Do I need an account?

No. The tool is free and needs no signup; it runs on your device and your files aren't uploaded.

How many images can I add?
