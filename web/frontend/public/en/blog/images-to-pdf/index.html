Turn Photos and Scans Into a Single PDF: Free, in Your Browser

Ten loose photos are awkward to send. One ordered PDF is not. Here is how to build it, and how to avoid the problems that make people send it twice.

Whether it is photographed paperwork for an application, scanned receipts for expenses or pages for your archive, a single PDF is easier to send, print and keep than a pile of images.

Why a PDF beats separate images

One file with ordered pages looks more professional than several JPG or PNG attachments, prints more easily, and the recipient opens everything with one click.

Three steps

  1. Add images: Drag JPG or PNG files into the Image to PDF tool, up to 80 MB.
  2. Set the order: Arrange the images; each becomes a page.
  3. Download: Click Convert to PDF and the file is ready immediately.

Image to PDF — Combine photos and scans into one PDF for free.

The tool runs entirely in your browser. Your images stay on your device and are never uploaded.

Check the photos first

Images go into the PDF exactly as they are, because the tool does not repair the photo. Quality is decided when you take the shot, and a quick check is faster than sending the file twice.

  • Keep all four edges of the document in frame; a cut-off edge gets files rejected.
  • Avoid shadows and glare, and light the page from the front rather than the side.
  • Shoot straight on, because a skewed photo stays skewed in the PDF.
  • Make sure the text is legible; if you struggle on screen, so will the recipient.

If your photos are skewed or shadowed, the Document Scanner gives a better result than converting directly. It detects the edges and corrects perspective, so the output looks like it came from a scanner.

Mind the page order

Your gallery sorts by capture time, which often differs from the document's logical order, and fronts and backs of double-sided paperwork get mixed up most. Review the order once from start to finish before converting.

When the file is too large

One photo from a recent phone can be 3 to 5 MB, so ten pages reach 30 to 50 MB and exceed the 20 to 25 MB attachment limit of most email services. Do not degrade the shots; run the finished PDF through the compress tool instead, where gains on scanned material are usually substantial.

Making the text searchable

In a PDF built from images the text is a picture, so you cannot search or copy it. Run the PDF through OCR to add an invisible text layer. Ctrl+F then works and the appearance stays the same.

JPG or PNG?

JPG suits photographed paperwork and keeps files small. For screenshots, charts and sharp lines, PNG looks cleaner because its compression is lossless, at the cost of size. Mixing both in one PDF is fine.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will the order of images change in the PDF?

The order you set in the tool is preserved, and each image becomes its own page. You can drag to reorder after uploading and check it before downloading.

The PDF came out very large. What should I do?

Phone photos are several MB each, so the total grows fast. Running the finished PDF through the compress tool cuts the size noticeably without hurting legibility.

Can I search the text in the PDF afterwards?

Not directly; on a page built from an image the text is a picture. Run it through OCR to add an invisible text layer and search will work.

Can images of different sizes go into the same PDF?

Yes. Each image becomes its own page, so pages may differ in size. If you plan to print, resizing the images to a common size first gives a tidier result.

How do I convert images to PDF?

Add JPG or PNG files to the Image to PDF tool, set the order and download one PDF. Each image becomes a page.

Do I need an account?

No. The tool is free and needs no signup. It runs on your device and your files are not uploaded.

How many images can I add?

As many as you like, up to 80 MB in total.
