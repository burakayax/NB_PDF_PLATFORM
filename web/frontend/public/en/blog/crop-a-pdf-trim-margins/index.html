How to Crop a PDF: Select an Area, Save It as PDF or Image

Most of the time you do not need to shrink a page. You need one table, one question or one signature box out of it. Here is how to cut exactly that out, and how it differs from trimming the page box.

When people search for how to crop a PDF, they usually want one of two things: to cut away white margins, or to pull one specific part out of a page. Both come down to the same move. You draw a box over what you want to keep and save only that. Everything runs in your browser, so the file stays on your device.

Where cropping a PDF is useful

  • Sharing one table or chart without the rest of the page.
  • Pulling a single question out of an exam paper.
  • Sending only the relevant part of a receipt or invoice.
  • Leaving the wide empty borders of a scan out of the result.

Step by step

  1. Open the PDF: Drop it into the Snip PDF tool. The first page appears on screen and you can move between pages.
  2. Draw the box: Drag across the page. You can move the box and resize it from its corners, and zoom the window to fine-tune the edges.
  3. Click Add snip: The area goes into a list. Add more from this or other pages and reorder them in the list.
  4. Pick a format and download: Save as PDF, PNG or JPEG. Choose 2x or 3x resolution if it will be printed.

Snip PDF to Image — Select the area you want and save it as a PDF or an image, on your device, free.

Three ways to save the PDF

  • All one after another: snips are placed on A4 pages in order, in one or two columns. Two columns suit narrow snips and save paper. The result is a printable worksheet.
  • One snip per page: a single PDF where each snip sits on its own page at its own size.
  • Each snip as a separate PDF file: every snip becomes its own PDF. With more than one they arrive together in a ZIP; a single snip downloads as a plain PDF. This is the one to pick when each piece goes to a different person.

Snipping versus trimming the page box

Some programs crop by shrinking the page's CropBox. The trimmed part stays inside the file and is only hidden, so text in it can still be found and copied. Snipping works differently: the selected area is drawn as an image and only that area reaches the output. What you left out never makes it into the file.

That cuts both ways. It is reassuring when you share part of a document. The trade-off is that the snip is a picture, so its text cannot be selected or searched. If you need the text, run the result through Searchable PDF afterwards.

Resolution and file size

Higher resolution gives a sharper snip and a bigger file. 2x is enough for most jobs and prints cleanly. 3x helps with fine print in tables. 1x is only for something viewed on a screen. If the file grows too large to email, compress the result with Compress PDF.

On a phone

It works in a phone browser with nothing to install. Drag with a finger and adjust from the corners, and zoom in when you need a precise edge.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I crop a PDF?

Upload it, drag a box over the part of the page you want, click Add snip and save as PDF, PNG or JPEG. It runs on your device.

Can I take several areas from one page?

Yes. Select areas one after another and add each as its own snip. You can also add snips from different pages.

Can I get each crop as its own PDF file?

Yes. In the PDF layout choose Each snip as a separate PDF file. With several snips they download together as a ZIP.

Is the cropped-away part still in the file?

No. The selected area is drawn as an image and anything outside it does not reach the output. The flip side is that the text in a snip is an image and cannot be copied.

Can I copy text from a snip?

Not directly, because a snip is an image. Save it as a PDF and add a text layer with the Searchable PDF tool to make it searchable and copyable.

Is my file uploaded?

No. Selecting, snipping and saving all happen in your browser. The file is never sent over the internet.
