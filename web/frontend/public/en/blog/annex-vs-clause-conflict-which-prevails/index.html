When a Contract Annex Contradicts the Main Text

The body says 45 days. The annex says 30. A single 'annexes prevail' sentence decides which one you are held to, and when your penalty starts.

Long contracts say the same thing in several places: the main text, the technical specification, the price schedule. Most of the time they agree. When they do not, the question that matters is which one wins, and the answer often sits in a short precedence clause that few people read closely.

A conflict with a price tag

Clause 4 allows 45 days for delivery. The technical specification in Annex 1 says 30. Clause 13 reads: if the contract and its annexes conflict, the annexes prevail. The deadline is therefore 30 days. Deliver on day 45 and you are 15 days late, with the daily penalty already running.

The three situations you will meet

  • 'Annexes prevail': a detail in an annex can override the main text, so annexes deserve the same attention as the body.
  • 'The main text prevails': conflicting annex terms do not apply, though cleaning up the conflict is still safer.
  • No precedence clause: which one applies becomes a matter of argument, and that usually means a dispute.

A three-step way to find conflicts

  1. List the subjects: Note what the body fixes with numbers or names: period, price, penalty, payment term, parties, jurisdiction.
  2. Search the annexes for each one: Read how the same subject appears there. Figures, days and rates are where the differences are.
  3. Find the ranking clause: Locate the sentence saying which document wins, and apply it to every difference you found.

Before you sign

  1. Send the other side a written list of every conflicting place and agree on one figure for each.
  2. Rewrite the ranking clause into a balanced one, for example that the later-dated or more specific document prevails.
  3. Ask for and read any annex whose text you have not seen. An annex you were never shown can still bind you.

Frequently Asked Questions

What if there is no precedence clause?

Which provision applies is then open to interpretation and dispute. Adding a precedence clause before signing is the safest way to close that gap.

What if I have not seen an annex?

Ask for it. If the contract refers to the annex and the annex prevails, a document you have not read can bind you.
