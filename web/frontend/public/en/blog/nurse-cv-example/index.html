Nurse CV Example

In a nurse CV, write results rather than a duty list. Summary, bullet and skills examples inside.

The most common mistake in a nurse CV is listing duties instead of results. Employers want to see how well you did the job, not just that you were assigned it. This guide shows how to write the profile summary, work-experience bullets and skills section for a nurse role, with examples you can copy and adapt.

Order and length

Put your name, title and contact details at the top, a short profile summary beneath, then work experience, education and skills. With little experience (recent graduate, internship) move education and projects ahead of work history. One page is enough for most applications; a second page is acceptable beyond roughly ten years of experience.

Applicant tracking systems (ATS) read plain, single-column CVs with standard headings (Summary, Experience, Education, Skills) best. Pick a more designed template for the version you hand to a recruiter.

Profile summary examples for a nurse

Keep it to two to four sentences: years of experience, strengths and goal. Take one draft below and replace each [X] with your own information:

  • Nurse who puts patient safety and empathy first, experienced in fast-paced settings, team communication and accurate records.
  • I have [X] years of clinical experience and I am strong in emergency response and patient education.
  • I approach patients and their families calmly, clearly and with compassion, following protocols closely and working in coordination with my team.
  • I have worked in the [unit] unit and I am meticulous about medication administration, patient monitoring and infection control.
  • A healthcare professional who can make the right decision under pressure and keeps learning and developing.

Work-experience bullets for a nurse

Start each bullet with an action and, where you can, include a number: how many people, items, what percentage, how fast. Wherever you see [X] below, insert your own real figure; if you don't know it, leave that bullet out.

  • Cared for and monitored an average of [X] patients per day
  • Administered medication and treatment according to protocol
  • Educated patients and families on care
  • Responded to emergencies in coordination with the team
  • Kept patient records complete and timely
  • Improved compliance with infection-control measures
  • Measured and recorded vital signs regularly and reported to the physician on time
  • Performed nursing procedures such as dressings, injections and IV access
  • Handed over patient status completely and clearly at shift changes
  • Prepared and monitored patients before and after surgery
  • Supported the clinical training of new nurses and students
  • Provided care that respects patient rights and privacy
  • Kept complete records and ensured compliance in quality and patient-safety audits

Only write what you actually did. If you couldn't explain a bullet in an interview, it shouldn't be on your CV.

Skills section for a nurse

Mirror the job ad's wording: if it says "Excel", write "Excel" rather than "office software". These are commonly requested for this role; select only what you genuinely know:

  • Patient Care
  • Emergency Response
  • Medication Administration
  • Patient Education
  • Medical Records
  • Infection Control
  • Basic Life Support (BLS)

Checklist before you send it

  1. Fill in the numbers: No [X] placeholder should remain anywhere.
  2. Check the dates: No overlaps or unexplained gaps; the Analysis tab in CV Maker calculates this for you.
  3. Tailor to the ad: Where true, use the ad's keywords; the job-ad matcher shows which are missing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where should I put licences and certifications?

Add a separate "Licences and Certifications" heading right below Education, with the issuing body, date and (if any) expiry. Never put confidential patient information such as names or diagnoses on your CV.

How long should a nurse CV be?

One page is enough for most applications. With a long work history you can go to two, but the most important content (summary, latest role, key skills) should be on the first page.

What should a nurse CV include if I have no experience?

Lead with education, internships and projects, volunteering and certificates. For each, say what you did and the result. Don't add experience or figures you don't have.
