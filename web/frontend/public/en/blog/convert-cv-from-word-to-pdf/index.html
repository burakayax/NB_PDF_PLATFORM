How to Convert a CV (Résumé) From Word to PDF the Right Way

Why you should send your CV as a PDF for job applications, and how to convert it from Word without breaking the formatting — step by step.

A CV is the first document an employer sees about you. A résumé built in Word can shift on different computers and its fonts can change. The fix is simple: convert it to PDF before sending.

Why should a CV be sent as a PDF?

  • Looks the same on every device: Fonts, line spacing and layout are preserved regardless of who opens it.
  • Professional standard: Most employers and career portals prefer, or even require, PDF.
  • Won't change by accident: A Word file can shift when opened; a PDF is fixed.

Convert a Word CV to PDF — step by step

  1. Upload your Word file: Drag and drop your .doc or .docx résumé into the Word-to-PDF tool.
  2. Convert: Click "Convert"; the PDF is prepared instantly with fonts, tables and layout preserved.
  3. Download and name it: Download the PDF and save it with a clean name like "First_Last_CV.pdf".

Final checks before you send

  • Is the file name professional? Use "First_Last_CV.pdf" instead of "document1.pdf".
  • Do the fonts and alignment in the PDF match the Word version?
  • Are the photo and links (LinkedIn, email) in the right place?
  • If the file is too big, shrink it to email size with "Compress PDF".

If you're asked to send several documents (CV + cover letter + certificates) as one file, convert them to PDF and then combine them with "Merge PDF".

How application systems read your CV

At most large employers and career portals your CV is read by software first, and reaching human eyes depends on that. These systems parse the text in the document, so your PDF must contain real text. A CV exported as an image from a design tool, or built from a screenshot, is a blank page to the system. There is a simple test: open the PDF and try to select your name with the cursor; if you cannot, it is an image.

Layouts that make automatic reading harder

  • Two-column designs: reading order can scramble and details run together.
  • Burying information in text boxes or tables.
  • Putting contact details in the page header — some systems never read headers.
  • Showing skill levels only as bars or stars; information with no text equivalent is not recorded.
  • Unusual section headings; a creative title instead of «Experience» makes matching harder.

Cleaning up on the way from Word to PDF

A PDF freezes the Word document as it stands, so before converting you should turn off track changes, accept all revisions and delete comments — otherwise those traces can end up in the output. Review the author name in document properties too; if you took the template from someone else, another name may still be sitting there.

File name and sending

A recruiter downloads dozens of files a day, and a document called «cv_final_v3.pdf» gets lost in the folder. A name in the form First_Last_CV is easier to find and looks considered. When a cover letter and certificates are requested, merging them into one PDF is tidier than separate attachments, and page numbers show the document arrived complete.

A last look before sending

  • Do fonts and alignment look the same as in Word?
  • Are links (LinkedIn, portfolio) clickable and pointing at the right address?
  • Is the file size reasonable — compress it if a photo inflated it?
  • Is there an old employer name or a draft note left in that you did not want shown?
  • Open it on a phone once; many recruiters take their first look there.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should a CV be?

The common expectation is one page for a few years of experience and two for longer careers. Bringing relevant experience forward beats adding material to fill a page.

Should I send PDF or Word?

Unless the posting says otherwise, send PDF so the layout holds on every device. Some systems explicitly ask for Word, in which case follow the instruction.

Should I include a photo?

Expectations differ by country and sector, and there is no single rule. If you include one, keep it small enough not to inflate the file.

Should I password-protect my PDF?

Do not. A password-protected CV cannot be opened by an application system and can get you screened out.

Does converting a Word CV to PDF break the formatting?

No. The Word-to-PDF tool preserves fonts, tables and layout, so your CV looks the same on every device.

Is converting a CV to PDF free?

Yes. Upload your Word document and create and download the PDF instantly.

What should I name the file?

Use a clear name like "First_Last_CV.pdf"; employers find it professional and can locate your file easily.

Can I combine my CV and cover letter into one PDF?

Yes. After converting both to PDF, combine them into one document with the "Merge PDF" tool.
