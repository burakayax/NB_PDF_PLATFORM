How to Read a Tender Document (RFP): What to Watch For

Missing a few key clauses in a long tender document is costly. Here's how AI makes an RFP readable in seconds.

Tender documents are long, technical and dense; yet winning or being disqualified often hinges on a few critical clauses. This post covers what to always check and how to speed up the process.

What to always check in a tender

  • Parties: the awarding body and what's expected from the bidder.
  • Scope: exactly what's required and delivery terms.
  • Required documents and qualification criteria (experience, capacity).
  • Deposit amount and type (bid/performance bond).
  • Submission deadline and time — miss it and you're out.
  • Withdrawal penalty, late penalties and termination clauses.
  • Evaluation criteria: lowest price or most economically advantageous?

In what order should you read a tender?

Experienced bid writers do not read a tender end to end; they first decide whether to bid at all. The information behind that decision sits in specific places in the document.

  • Qualification criteria first: if you do not meet the experience, turnover or capacity requirements, reading the rest is pointless.
  • Then the submission deadline: is the remaining preparation time realistic?
  • Then the scope: can you deliver the work, or will you need a subcontractor?
  • Then bonds and payment terms: does your cash flow support them?
  • Technical detail last: go there only once you have decided to bid.

Typical mistakes that get bids disqualified

The most frustrating way to lose a tender is not on price but on a formality. These failures have nothing to do with the substance of the bid and are entirely preventable.

  • A required document missing or out of date.
  • A bid bond whose amount or validity does not match the tender.
  • The bid not submitted in the required form (sealed envelope, digitally signed file).
  • The total on the unit price schedule not matching the figure in the bid letter.
  • Missing the cut-off time — last-minute uploads to online systems are risky.

Track addenda and clarifications

After a tender is published, the awarding body may issue clarifications or amendments. These usually affect dates, technical specifications or the list of required documents, and they take precedence over the original text. A bid prepared against the old version can be invalid even if it is otherwise correct. Extracting the difference between two versions with a comparison tool is both faster and more reliable than reading by hand.

Sharing the tender with your team

The technical section concerns engineering, the financial section concerns accounting, and the administrative section concerns the bid manager. Rather than sending a forty-page file to everyone, splitting out the relevant page ranges raises the chance it gets read and speeds up responses. Attaching the summary as well lets the team grasp the context without opening the whole document.

Keep records

Tender processes can be audited later. Keeping the version you downloaded, every addendum and all correspondence with their dates in a single folder lets you answer the question of which version you bid against in minutes. Merging the documents into one PDF and adding page numbers keeps that archive tidy.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does downloading a tender oblige me to bid?

Usually not; downloading the documents is not a commitment to bid. Some processes do record who took the documents and send notices to that list, so it is worth following announcements after downloading.

Can I summarize a scanned tender?

Yes. Text is extracted with OCR first and then summarized. If the scan quality is poor, verify amounts and dates against the original.

How do I find a clause the summary did not show?

Asking the document directly is the fastest route, because the answer rests on its own text. Alternatively, make the document searchable and search by keyword.

Can I compare several tenders?

Producing a summary for each and placing them side by side works well in practice. With many files, batch processing gathers the summaries into one document and makes comparison easier.

What matters most in a tender document?

Submission deadline, deposit, required documents/qualifications, penalties and evaluation criteria. Missing these can disqualify you.

Can AI summarize a tender?

Yes. The AI Summarize tool recognizes tenders and surfaces critical clauses like parties, deposit, deadline and penalties.
