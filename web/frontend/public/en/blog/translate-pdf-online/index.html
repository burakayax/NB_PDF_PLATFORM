Translate a PDF Into Another Language and Keep the Layout

Pasting a PDF into a translation page destroys its structure. Here is a way to translate that keeps headings, lists and tables, and an honest list of its limits.

Reading a contract, paper or manual in a language you do not know is a common problem, and most solutions wreck the formatting on the way. This guide covers a method that keeps the document's structure intact, plus the cases where machine translation is not enough.

What goes wrong with copy and paste

Copy text into a translation page and headings, lists and tables vanish. On long documents the result becomes unmanageable, and numbers, dates and proper nouns have to survive untouched.

Where a human translator is still required

AI translation is excellent for speed and understanding. Anything that needs legal weight, such as a notary, a court or an official application, requires a sworn translator. Use AI for a fast first reading and a draft.

To grasp what a long document is about before translating, generate a summary first with the AI Summarize tool.

Prepare the file first

Translation works on the text taken from the document, so clean extraction means accurate translation. In a scan, skewed pages and shadows reduce recognition, and every word lost there is missing from the translation. Straightening pages and making the document searchable first is the cheapest quality gain you can make.

Leave these untranslated

  • Names of people, organizations and brands.
  • Addresses, especially if they will be used for international post.
  • Product codes, serial numbers and reference numbers.
  • Names of legislation and clause numbers.
  • Currency abbreviations and technical units.

Checking these first when you review a translation catches the easiest errors.

Be careful with technical and legal text

Field-specific terms can differ from everyday meanings. In law, indemnity, warranty and liability map differently between legal systems, and in technical writing the same word is read differently across industries. Use the translation to understand, and for a binding decision go back to the original and, if needed, a specialist.

Keep original and translation together

When working across languages, merge the original and the translation into one document. You can then point to the text the other side sees while you discuss a clause, which is the most practical way for a team to be sure everyone means the same thing.

What to do next

  • Summarize a long document for its key points.
  • Ask the document directly about a clause you do not follow.
  • Extract the differences if you have two versions.
  • Remove unnecessary personal data before sharing the translation.
