How to Convert PDF to JPG / Images: One Image per Page

When you need to share a PDF page as an image, put it on the web, or drop it into a slide, the cleanest way is to convert it to JPG/PNG. Here's how — and whether to pick JPG or PNG.

You can't embed a PDF everywhere — but you can embed an image. To share a page on social media, place it on a web page, or add it to a slide, converting the PDF to JPG or PNG is the most practical route.

When is it useful?

  • Sharing a single page as an image on social media.
  • Adding a preview to a website or blog post.
  • Dropping a page into a presentation as a picture.
  • Quickly showing a page to someone who can't open a PDF.

JPG or PNG?

JPG is smaller and ideal for photo-heavy pages. PNG keeps sharp edges and text crisper and supports transparency — prefer it for logos, diagrams or text-heavy pages.

Why resolution matters

Converting a PDF page to an image means turning a vector document into pixels, and resolution decides the quality of that result with no way back: an image produced at low resolution cannot be sharpened afterwards. Roughly, medium resolution suits on-screen sharing, high suits presentations and the web, and the highest available suits print.

Why a screenshot is not enough

Taking a screenshot looks like the fastest way to share a page, but the result is limited to your screen's resolution and usually comes out soft. It also picks up the frame, margins and the viewer's interface. Converting the PDF directly gives a clean output at the page's own resolution, with straight edges and crisp text.

Practical notes for social media and the web

  • A portrait A4 page gets cropped in most social feeds; snipping the section you want first looks much better.
  • Compressing an image before putting it on a web page noticeably improves load time.
  • PNG works better for text-heavy pages, JPG for photo-heavy ones.
  • Text in a converted page is no longer text; search engines cannot read it. Write important wording on the page as well.

The whole page, or part of it?

Often what you need is not the whole page but a table, chart or paragraph on it. Snipping the area directly is faster than converting the page and cropping it in an image editor, and it preserves resolution. When preparing presentations and reports, this is the method that helps most often.

One side effect of converting to an image

When a page becomes an image its text layer disappears: it can no longer be copied, searched or read by screen readers. Sometimes that is exactly what you want, if you would rather the text not be lifted easily. But where accessibility matters, or where the document will be archived, keep the PDF itself and use the image only as a sharing copy.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many pages can I convert?

There is no hard page limit; since each page becomes its own image, long documents arrive as an archive file. If you only need a few pages, splitting first is more practical.

Can I get a transparent background?

PNG supports transparency, but if the PDF page has a white background the output is white too. For transparency you need to process the image separately in an editor.

Why are the image files bigger than the PDF?

A PDF stores text as vectors and takes very little space, while an image records every pixel. So the image of a text-heavy page can be larger than its source.

Does converting a scanned PDF to an image make sense?

A scanned page is already an image, so converting it again adds a second round of loss. For such documents, extracting the embedded images directly gives a better result.

How do I convert a PDF to JPG?

Upload the PDF to the tool; each page is converted into a separate JPG/PNG image. Download them individually or as a ZIP.

Will image quality drop?

Pages are processed at high resolution for crisp results on screen and in print. For sharper text, prefer PNG.

Can I convert just one page?

Since each page becomes a separate image, you can pick and download the image for the page you want.
